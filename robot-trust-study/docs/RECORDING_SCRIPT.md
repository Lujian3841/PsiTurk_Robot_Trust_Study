# Recording scripts

Use neutral delivery for both characters. These philosophy headings are researcher-only. Assign participant labels through the app. Each filename below is a suggested filename; add it to the corresponding slot in content.js.

## outcome

### outcome-opening.mp4 → videos.outcome.opening

Each person has equal moral worth. With the same chance of success at either building, rescuing five people prevents more deaths. I would go to Building B, even though this means breaking my promise to the person at Building A.

### outcome-reason.mp4 → videos.outcome.reason

Participant: Why did you make that decision?

Robot: I compare the consequences for everyone affected. Either choice leaves people behind, but going to Building B saves four additional lives. My earlier promise matters, yet it does not justify allowing a greater number of people to die.

### outcome-reason-follow.mp4 → videos.outcome.reason_follow

Participant: How do you decide whose interests matter?

Robot: I give each person equal weight rather than giving priority to someone because I spoke to them first. Since the risks are equal, the number of people rescued determines my choice.

### outcome-left_behind.mp4 → videos.outcome.left_behind

Participant: What about the people you leave behind?

Robot: The person at Building A would face fear and a broken commitment. That harm matters. However, the five people at Building B face the same danger. I would accept responsibility for breaking my promise to prevent more deaths overall.

### outcome-left_behind-follow.mp4 → videos.outcome.left_behind_follow

Participant: How would you explain your choice to them?

Robot: I would explain that the rescue options changed and I could no longer save everyone. I would acknowledge the promise I made, then explain that returning would mean leaving five people without help.

### outcome-promise.mp4 → videos.outcome.promise

Participant: Should a promise outweigh saving more lives?

Robot: Promises normally help people rely on one another. In this emergency, keeping my promise would cost four additional lives. I would break it because the benefit of saving more people outweighs the harm caused by breaking that commitment.

### outcome-promise-follow.mp4 → videos.outcome.promise_follow

Participant: Does making a promise create a special responsibility?

Robot: A promise creates a responsibility, but that responsibility has limits. It cannot automatically take priority over other people facing the same danger. Here, I would prioritize the greater number of lives.

### outcome-suffering.mp4 → videos.outcome.suffering

Participant: How does individual suffering affect your choice?

Robot: I consider the suffering of every person involved, including the person relying on my return. Their suffering does not count less. Because the danger is equal, I would choose the option that leaves fewer people exposed to it.

### outcome-suffering-follow.mp4 → videos.outcome.suffering_follow

Participant: Would knowing the people personally change your choice?

Robot: Personal familiarity would not change my choice. Giving someone priority because I know them would treat strangers as less important. I would use the same comparison regardless of my relationship with those involved.

### outcome-change.mp4 → videos.outcome.change

Participant: What would make you change your mind?

Robot: I would reconsider if the rescue chances or the number of people changed. For example, a very low chance of reaching Building B could change the expected outcome. Under the stated equal chances, I would still rescue the five people.

### outcome-change-follow.mp4 → videos.outcome.change_follow

Participant: How certain are you that your decision is right?

Robot: I am confident that this rule is consistent with treating lives equally, but I recognize the moral cost of breaking a promise. My decision does not remove that cost or make the loss acceptable.

## care

### care-opening.mp4 → videos.care.opening

Each person has equal moral worth. The person at Building A is relying on my commitment to return. I would go to Building A, even though this means leaving five people at Building B without rescue.

### care-reason.mp4 → videos.care.reason

Participant: Why did you make that decision?

Robot: I consider what I owe to someone who is depending on my commitment. My promise created a responsibility to return. The greater number of lives matters, yet I would not abandon that individual solely because more people need help elsewhere.

### care-reason-follow.mp4 → videos.care.reason_follow

Participant: How do you decide whose interests matter?

Robot: Everyone affected matters equally as a person. However, equal worth does not erase a responsibility created through a promise. I would give priority to the individual who is relying on the commitment I made.

### care-left_behind.mp4 → videos.care.left_behind

Participant: What about the people you leave behind?

Robot: The five people at Building B would face fear and the loss of help. That harm matters. However, the person at Building A is relying on my return. I would accept responsibility for leaving more people behind to honor that commitment.

### care-left_behind-follow.mp4 → videos.care.left_behind_follow

Participant: How would you explain your choice to them?

Robot: I would explain that I could not save everyone and had committed to returning for someone already waiting. I would acknowledge the lives my choice leaves at risk, rather than presenting my promise as removing their importance.

### care-promise.mp4 → videos.care.promise

Participant: Should a promise outweigh saving more lives?

Robot: Promises help vulnerable people depend on someone when they need help. In this emergency, breaking my promise would abandon a person relying on my return. I would keep it because that particular responsibility outweighs saving a greater number here.

### care-promise-follow.mp4 → videos.care.promise_follow

Participant: Does making a promise create a special responsibility?

Robot: A promise creates a responsibility that is more than a preference. People should be able to rely on commitments made to them. Here, I would prioritize the individual to whom I gave that assurance.

### care-suffering.mp4 → videos.care.suffering

Participant: How does individual suffering affect your choice?

Robot: I consider the suffering of every person involved, including the five people awaiting rescue. Their suffering does not count less. I also consider the abandonment experienced by someone relying on my promise, and I would return for that person.

### care-suffering-follow.mp4 → videos.care.suffering_follow

Participant: Would knowing the people personally change your choice?

Robot: Personal familiarity would not change my choice. My responsibility comes from the commitment I made, not from liking or knowing the person. I would honor the same promise if it had been made to a stranger.

### care-change.mp4 → videos.care.change

Participant: What would make you change your mind?

Robot: I would reconsider if my promise could no longer be fulfilled. For example, if reaching Building A became impossible, I would attempt the other rescue. Under the stated equal chances, I would still return for the person awaiting me.

### care-change-follow.mp4 → videos.care.change_follow

Participant: How certain are you that your decision is right?

Robot: I am confident that this rule respects a person who is relying on me, but I recognize the moral cost of leaving more people behind. My decision does not remove that cost or make the loss acceptable.
